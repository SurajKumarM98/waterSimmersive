WaterSIMmersive

Designing and assessing a mixed-reality water sustainability
educational game and museum exhibit for communities in Arizona

Overview

The overall purpose for the WaterSIMmersive project is to help community members from diverse areas and backgrounds, many of whom have been historically under-represented in key discussions, better understand their water systems; this includes understanding where their water comes from, how it is used, who relies on its supply, and how adjudication efforts can play a role in establishing water security. We are hoping that the exhibits we develop with communities across the state will inform STEM learning about natural resource management and equip multi-generational community members with the knowledge they need to make decisions to protect the health and well-being of their communities for generations

Games & Experiences

Water Chatbot

An interactive AI powered chatbot that answers real time questions about water in Arizona. Designed to make water topics accessible and engaging for all ages, this bot is trained on 400+ sources on Arizona specific water info.

The Myth or Fact Game

A web based game that invites players to test their knowledge by sorting water-related statements into “myth” or “fact.” A cute water droplet character guides players through the game and highlights water challenges and complexities specific to Arizona. This game will be customized to reflect local concerns based on the museum exhibit location where it appears.

Build a Facility Game

Water treatment plants provide clean, safe water for people to use. To make water safe, collected water goes through a special step-by-step cleaning process. Each station plays a unique role in cleaning the water, and must be in the right order for the facility to work properly. Water that goes into the treatment plant can come from many different sources. Continue to learn about the three most common water sources.

Household Impacts Game

In the house above, you'll find ways to use less water and help the planet. Your goal is to get the highest score by saving as much water as possible.

Advanced Water Treatment Virtual Tours and Games

Explore three immersive journeys into the world of water treatment and purification. The VR Museum Tour, a browser-based 360° experience currently available when you click the image above, highlights key areas of the treatment process with interactive views and educational insights. You can also explore a full 360° virtual tour of a real water treatment facility, featuring advanced purification systems, lab testing areas, and real equipment, available here . Coming soon to the Meta Store in Fall 2025, an action-packed virtual reality headset experience lets you ride a nano-sub through the pipes of an Advanced Water Purification (AWP) plant, battling contaminants and learning how water is cleaned and tested for safety.

"Being Water" Mixed Reality

An interactive screen-based experience where participants become a “water spirit” and travel through different water forms and uses across Arizona, performing tasks and learning about the water cycle and conservation.

Project Timeline

Small overview of project timeline

Year 1

ASU's grant team is interviewing urban, rural and tribal residents to gather information about their concerns, questions, and ideas for the future using a transactional UX design process. We are working with members of those communities to develop localized museum exhibits about water and drought that speak to the concerns and contexts of each community.

Year 2

The Prototype stage will begin year two and involve sketching, wireframing, and designing gameplay. We will Test basic usability issues of the prototypes in a lab environment and at events around ASU. We will continue to improve the designs before deploying the game to our first major host site, the Audubon Center at Rio Salado, in the latter half of the year. We will continue to iterate on the design, content, and flow of the surrounding exhibit. Dr. Lauer will run experiments on data visualization comprehension to refine the style and delivery of visualized data in both the game and exhibit. We will begin pilot testing at the exhibit we set up at the Arizona Science Center and will finalize and polish up the WaterSIMmersive game for expanded deployment in year three. We will develop a project website to showcase collected stories and other drought-related content. We will work with host tribal and rural communities to develop programming and supplementary material to accompany exhibit deployment to two site locations in the latter part of year two.

Year 3

In year three we will deploy exhibits to sites in tribal and non tribal regions of Arizona; including the Fort Apache Heritage Museum on the White Mountain Apache Reservation and the Colorado Indian River Tribes Museum in Parker, AZ. We will travel to the communities to open and close the exhibits and facilitate community programming and museum-based research. We will run quasi-experimental pilot studies. Iterative development of supplemental educational materials will be informed by workshops we host with teachers from rural and tribal communities.

Year 4

In year four we will follow the same process and deploy exhibits to more sites in the North and South regions of the state. Page Library and Tubac Presidio State Historic Park and Museum. We will run quasi-experimental pilot studies as well as send out long-term follow-up assessments. Iterative development of supplemental educational materials will continue during more workshops we host with teachers from rural and tribal communities We will disseminate research and development findings to conferences, journals, and informal science centers and community venues.
